For an easy run in 30 degree weather with little wind, wear shorts or half tights, a long-sleeve tech tee, a headband and light gloves, because your body heat makes it feel like about 45°F once you're moving. If there's a breeze, move up to running tights, a base layer with a half-zip, gloves and an ear cover.

Thirty degrees is where wind starts to decide your outfit. The thermometer reading matters less than the feels-like temperature and how hard you plan to run. Here's how to work it out.

## The +15°F Rule at 30 Degrees

Running makes heat. A lot of it. That's why experienced runners dress for a temperature 15–20°F warmer than the forecast. [Should I Run](/) takes this further and adjusts the offset by effort level.

On a calm 30°F day, where the feels-like temperature is close to the air temperature:

| Effort | Heat Offset | Dress-For Temp at 30°F |
|--------|------------|----------------------|
| Easy | +15°F | 45°F |
| Endurance | +17°F | 47°F |
| Tempo | +20°F | 50°F |
| Threshold | +22°F | 52°F |
| Interval | +25°F | 55°F |

Easy through threshold all land in the same tier. Intervals tip you into the next lighter one.

## What to Wear: The 30°F Tier

For an **easy run** on a calm 30°F day (dressing for about 45°F), you're at the cold edge of the 45–54°F tier:

**Bottom:** Shorts or half tights. Capris or joggers work if you prefer more coverage. At the bottom of this tier, many runners pick half tights or full tights, and that's a reasonable call.

**Top:** Long-sleeve tech tee. A lightweight half-zip or thin hoodie is a good alternative. One breathable layer that wicks sweat is the goal.

**Accessories:** A headband for your ears, and light gloves. The app lists gloves as optional at this tier, but at 30°F most people will want them for the first mile.

For **intervals** (dressing for about 55°F), you can drop to shorts and a T-shirt or light long sleeve, with arm sleeves you can push down as you warm up. Wear something warmer for the warm-up jog and take it off before the first rep.

## Wind Changes Everything

At 30°F, a little wind goes a long way. Using the NWS wind chill formula:

| Wind | Wind Chill at 30°F | Easy-Run Dress-For Temp | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | About 30°F | 45°F | 45–54°F |
| 10 mph | About 21°F | 36°F | 35–44°F |
| 15 mph | About 19°F | 34°F | 25–34°F |
| 20 mph | About 17°F | 32°F | 25–34°F |

A 10 mph wind is enough to move an easy run into the **35–44°F tier**: running tights, a long-sleeve base layer with a half-zip, gloves, and a headband or ear cover.

Over 15 mph, you drop into the **25–34°F tier**: running tights, a base layer with a midweight jacket (or fleece plus a wind shell), warm gloves, a beanie and a neck gaiter. Should I Run also adds a wind-resistant layer whenever wind is above 15 mph.

The feels-like number in your weather app may not match these figures exactly, because forecast providers calculate it in slightly different ways. The point is the size of the drop. For more on how wind affects you, read [running in the wind](/blog/running-in-wind/).

## The "Runs Hot" Factor

If you always end up shedding layers, turn on the "I run hot" setting. It adds +5°F to your dress-for temperature. On a calm 30°F easy run, that's 50°F instead of 45°F, which puts you comfortably in the middle of the long-sleeve-and-shorts tier rather than at the edge.

It's a good fit if you sweat heavily, run with a lot of body heat, or regularly find yourself overdressed at the halfway point.

## Common Mistakes at 30°F

1. **Wearing a heavy winter coat.** A jacket built for standing at a bus stop will cook you within a mile. You need layers that breathe.
2. **Ignoring the wind.** A calm 30°F and a windy 30°F are different days. Check the feels-like temperature, not just the air temperature.
3. **Dressing for the first five minutes.** You *should* feel a bit cold when you step outside. If you're cozy at the door, you'll be overheated by mile two.
4. **Leaving out hands and ears.** These cool fastest and hurt the most. Gloves and a headband are light and easy to stash.
5. **Forgetting the footing.** Thirty degrees is right at freezing, and puddles from yesterday can be ice today. Shorten your stride on shaded paths and bridges.

## FAQ

### Can I wear shorts running in 30 degree weather?

On a calm day at easy effort, yes. Your dress-for temperature is about 45°F, which is still in the shorts-or-half-tights range. In wind, switch to tights.

### Do I need a jacket at 30°F?

Usually not on a calm day. A long-sleeve tech tee or light half-zip is enough for most efforts. Once the wind is around 15 mph or more, a midweight jacket or a wind shell over a fleece makes sense.

### What should I wear on my head at 30 degrees?

A headband or ear cover on calm days. If the wind chill drops into the teens, switch to a beanie and add a neck gaiter.

### Is 30 degrees too cold to run?

No. A wind chill of 30°F is in the 0–2% slower pace range, so it barely affects performance. For where the real limits are, see [is it too cold to run](/blog/is-it-too-cold-to-run/).

## Bottom Line

At 30°F, start with a long-sleeve tech tee, shorts or half tights, light gloves and a headband for an easy run on a calm day. Add tights and a half-zip once there's a breeze, and a jacket or wind shell once it's over 15 mph. Wear less for harder efforts.
